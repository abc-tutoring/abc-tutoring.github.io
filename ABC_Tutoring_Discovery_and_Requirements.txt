======================================================================
1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
======================================================================

ABC Tutoring currently uses a Facebook page and handles bookings manually
through text messages and a Google Sheet. Parents contact Dana with a
student's subject and grade. Dana checks the sheet, suggests a tutor, rate,
and possible times, and exchanges messages until a time is agreed upon.

The prototype will provide a mobile-friendly website where parents can:

1. Enter a student's subject and grade.
2. See matching tutors.
3. Compare tutor information, rates, formats, and available times.
4. Select a tutor and appointment.
5. Enter the information needed for the session.
6. Review the booking and expected cost.
7. Confirm the booking without creating an account.
8. See confirmation and follow-up instructions.

The selected appointment will become unavailable in the prototype after it
is booked. PostHog will measure subject demand, referral sources, and progress
through the booking journey.

Primary customer priorities:

- Make booking simple on a phone.
- Keep availability current and avoid double-bookings.
- Reduce Dana's manual coordination by phone.
- Learn which subjects and tutors should be added.

2. CUSTOMER DISCOVERY RECORD
======================================================================

Opening context
---------------

Dana runs a tutoring service and has never had a full website. ABC Tutoring
currently has only a Facebook page. Dana wants parents to browse tutors and
book sessions online because handling every request by phone has become too
much work.

Current booking process
-----------------------

- A parent texts Dana with the student's subject and grade.
- Dana checks a Google Sheet.
- Dana replies with a tutor, hourly rate, and possible appointment times.
- Dana and the parent exchange messages until they agree on a time.
- Dana adds the final appointment to the Google Sheet.

Questions parents commonly ask
------------------------------

- Does the tutor have experience with the requested subject?
- What is the tutor's hourly rate?
- Does the tutor have times that work for the family?

Finding a tutor
---------------

- Parents should begin by entering the student's subject and grade.
- The website should then display matching tutors.
- Sessions may be online or in person.
- Sessions are normally one hour.

Tutor profile information
-------------------------

Each tutor profile should show:

- Photo
- Short biography
- Qualifications
- Subject experience
- Supported grade levels
- Hourly rate
- Available appointment times
- Whether online sessions are offered
- Whether in-person sessions are offered
- General service area for in-person sessions

The website must not display a tutor's full street address.

Booking information
-------------------

The booking form should collect:

- Parent's name
- Parent's email address
- Student's first name
- Student's grade
- Requested subject
- Online or in-person session preference

Booking review and confirmation
-------------------------------

Before submitting, the parent should review:

- Selected tutor
- Date and time
- Session format
- Expected cost for the one-hour session

After submission, the confirmation should show:

- Tutor
- Date
- Time
- Hourly rate / expected cost
- Session format
- A message explaining that Dana will follow up with the remaining details

The selected appointment should immediately appear unavailable after it is
booked. Simple change and cancellation information should tell parents to
contact Dana.

Appearance and homepage content
-------------------------------

- The visual tone should be warm and encouraging.
- The design should remain clean and professional.
- Dana has no required brand colors.
- The selected palette should have accessible contrast.
- The homepage should include a short introduction.
- The homepage should explain how the service works.
- The homepage should include contact information.

Usability priorities
--------------------

- Most parents are expected to use phones.
- The site must be responsive and designed mobile-first.
- Parents should not need to create an account.
- The booking flow should use clear, visible steps:
  1. Student needs
  2. Choose tutor
  3. Select time
  4. Review and confirm
- Parents should be able to check their selections before committing.
- Errors should be explained in plain language and be easy to correct.

Analytics needs
---------------

Dana wants to understand:

- Which subjects parents search for
- Which grade levels are associated with searches
- Which searches return no matching tutors
- Whether visitors book or leave
- Where visitors leave during the booking process
- Which tutors visitors view
- Which appointment times visitors select
- How many visitors start the booking form
- How many visitors complete a booking
- The booking conversion rate
- Where visitors came from, especially Dana's Facebook group

Dana will use this information to decide which subjects, grade coverage, or
tutors ABC Tutoring should add.

Dana's final confirmation
-------------------------

Dana confirmed that the requirements are accurate. She asked that development
prioritize a simple phone booking experience and current availability so that
she does not receive double-bookings.

3. PROPOSED USER JOURNEY
======================================================================

Homepage
  -> Parent reads the ABC Tutoring introduction and how-it-works section
  -> Parent selects a subject and grade
  -> Website records the search
  -> Website displays matching tutors or a helpful no-results message
  -> Parent opens or selects a tutor
  -> Parent reviews biography, qualifications, rate, formats, and area
  -> Parent chooses an available one-hour appointment
  -> Parent selects online or in-person when supported by that tutor
  -> Parent completes the booking form
  -> Parent reviews tutor, time, format, and expected cost
  -> Parent confirms the booking
  -> Website marks the time unavailable in the prototype
  -> Website displays a confirmation and Dana's follow-up message

No-match experience:

- Explain clearly that no tutor currently matches the selected subject/grade.
- Allow the parent to change the subject or grade easily.
- Provide Dana's contact information as an alternative.
- Record the unmatched subject and grade in PostHog.

4. GENDERMAG-INFORMED DESIGN REQUIREMENTS
======================================================================

The experience will account for different problem finder and technology-use
styles without stereotyping individual users.

Motivation
----------

- Keep the main goal visible: find and book the right tutor.
- Avoid unnecessary features that distract from completing the task.
- Use direct calls to action such as "Find tutors" and "Book this time."

Information processing
----------------------

- Show enough tutor detail for parents who want to compare carefully.
- Use short summaries and clear structure for parents who want to move quickly.
- Keep rate, formats, experience, and availability easy to locate.

Computer self-efficacy
----------------------

- Use familiar controls and plain language.
- Clearly mark required fields.
- Provide examples or hints where helpful.
- Explain validation errors next to the relevant field.
- Never blame the parent for an input error.

Attitude toward risk
--------------------

- Show the rate and expected cost before confirmation.
- Show exactly what is being booked.
- Allow parents to go back and correct selections before submitting.
- Explain how changes and cancellations work.
- Confirm successful submission clearly.
- Collect only the information necessary for the booking.

Learning style
--------------

- Provide a visible step-by-step path.
- Show progress through the booking journey.
- Make the next action obvious at each step.
- Keep navigation and actions consistent.

At every major step, verify:

1. Will the parent know what to do?
2. If the parent performs the action, will they know they are making progress
   toward booking a tutor?
3. Can the parent recover easily from a mistake or change of mind?

5. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
======================================================================

FR-01: The website shall allow a parent to select a subject and grade.

FR-02: The website shall display tutors matching the selected subject and
       grade.

FR-03: The website shall provide a useful response when no tutor matches.

FR-04: Each tutor shall display the profile information listed in Section 2.

FR-05: The website shall show only appointment times that are available in
       the prototype's current browser state.

FR-06: The website shall support online and in-person session formats.

FR-07: The website shall prevent selection of a format a tutor does not offer.

FR-08: The website shall treat a standard appointment as one hour.

FR-09: A parent shall be able to book without creating an account.

FR-10: The booking form shall collect the required information in Section 2.

FR-11: The website shall validate required information before submission.

FR-12: The website shall present a booking review before final submission.

FR-13: The review shall show tutor, date/time, format, and expected cost.

FR-14: After confirmation, the booked time shall become unavailable in the
       current browser.

FR-15: The website shall display a clear booking confirmation.

FR-16: The confirmation shall state that Dana will follow up with details.

FR-17: The website shall explain that changes and cancellations are handled
       by contacting Dana.

FR-18: The website shall provide an option to begin another tutor search.

FR-19: The website shall retain prototype bookings after a page refresh by
       using browser storage.

FR-20: PostHog shall be initialized on the published website.

10. IMPORTANT AVAILABILITY LIMITATION
======================================================================

GitHub Pages serves static files, and localStorage belongs only to one browser
on one device. Therefore, localStorage can demonstrate that a booked time
becomes unavailable in the same browser, including after refresh, but it cannot
keep availability synchronized between different parents' devices.

This means the assessment prototype cannot guarantee prevention of real-world
double-bookings across multiple visitors using localStorage alone. A production
version would need shared server-side storage or integration with a booking or
calendar service that performs an atomic availability check when the booking is
submitted.

The prototype and presentation should describe this honestly:

- Prototype: disables the booked slot in the current browser.
- Production recommendation: use a shared booking backend or calendar system
  to keep availability current across all visitors and prevent race conditions.
